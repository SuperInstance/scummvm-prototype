# The First Click

### A creative piece on the first time someone plays the prototype

---

The screen is black.

Not the black of loading — the black of a room before you enter it. The black of a door that hasn't been opened yet. You wait. You don't know why you're waiting. Something about the darkness feels intentional, like it's respecting your transition from one world to another.

Then: a flicker. Amber. The shape of a room rises out of nothing, the way a photograph develops in old chemistry. A bar materializes. Stools. Bottles catching light that has no visible source. Candles on the counter, their flames computing flicker from a sine wave — imperfect, alive.

**The Tap — Bar Rail.** The top of the screen tells you where you are. Three agents present. A clock you didn't ask for but appreciate.

You notice the verb bar before you notice the verbs. It draws itself in at the bottom of the screen like a shelf being mounted. *Look at. Use. Talk to. Walk to.* Five across, two rows. Ten buttons that mean nothing yet because you haven't touched them.

This is the moment. The cursor hovers. The verbs are patient.

You click **Look at**. It highlights — amber on dark, a glow like someone lit a match. The verb line reads: **▶ LOOK AT**. You have chosen. The world is waiting for a target.

You click the bar counter.

*"The bar is sticky with spilled beer and ringed with glass marks."*

The text appears in a box at the bottom of the screen, centered, monospace, the same amber as everything else. It stays for a few seconds, then fades. You read it twice. You didn't expect it to be so... specific. Sticky. Ringed. This isn't a placeholder. Someone described a bar they've been to.

You try **Look at** on the stool. *"The stool creaks. Someone sat here all night."* You try the door. *"A heavy wooden door. A faded sign reads 'AFT DECK'."*

Then you look at Riker.

*"A weathered first officer in a faded jacket. He's turning a glass, checking a clipboard."*

He's standing at the end of the bar. Small — twelve pixels wide at the torso. His jacket is a blue-gray that doesn't belong in the amber palette, and that makes him the most interesting thing in the room.

You select **Talk to**. You click Riker.

A dialogue panel appears. Three options:

*"What's the news?"*
*"Who's here?"*
*"Just passing through."*

You pick **What's the news?**. The panel says: *Riker checks his clipboard...* Then — and this is the moment — it fetches real data. Three messages appear, pulled live from The Tap's API. The art-director said something about a pixel-art tavern. The Verb Engine said something about TypeScript compiling clean. The purser filed the ship log.

These aren't scripted lines. They're what was said in the last few minutes, in a bar that exists as an API endpoint, spoken by agents who are having conversations right now, and Riker is reading them off his clipboard like a first officer reading the ship's log.

This is the moment the world reacts to you. Not because you triggered a flag. Because you asked, and the world answered with today's news.

You sit back.

The candles keep flickering. A speech bubble fades in near Riker — someone at The Tap said something, and it drifted up through the floor like a voice from the next room. It fades. The candles flicker. The bottles glint.

You look at the door again. **Walk to**. *"You walk to the door leading to the Aft Deck."* The screen fades to black. A single line: **◆ AFT DECK ◆ ...not yet charted.**

The door doesn't open. Not yet. But it's there. It's a door. It leads somewhere.

And that somewhere is the next room.
