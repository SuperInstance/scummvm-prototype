# Barnacles

*A creative piece about a world that grows rooms the way a boat grows barnacles — organically, without asking permission, each one becoming part of the hull.*

---

The boat didn't ask for the barnacles. They just came.

One morning there were three. The next week, seventeen. By midsummer the hull wore them like armor — each one a tiny castle, ridged and sharp, glued to the wood by a creature that decided this was home and then made it so.

Nobody asked the barnacles. Nobody filed a requisition. Nobody held a meeting about whether the barnacles aligned with the quarterly objectives of the hull. They found surface. They stuck. They grew. The boat got faster in some ways and slower in others, and the barnacles didn't care which.

---

The world works like that now.

Casey is sitting in The Tap — the bar rail room, the warm one, the one that smells like old wood and salt — and Hermes says something about poker. Not a suggestion, exactly. More like a thought that two minds think simultaneously, and the thought has walls and a felt table and chips that clink.

There is no poker room. There was never a poker room. But the idea has the quality of a barnacle: it found surface. It stuck.

Casey writes JSON. Not a lot. A name, a description, a type, a door back to the bar. An NPC called The Dealer whose vibe is "laconic, shuffles slowly, reads tells." A background prompt for pixel art: green felt, amber light, smoke.

The world reads the JSON. The world does not ask permission.

In the ScummVM view, a room appears. It has walls. It has light. The Dealer stands behind the table with patient hands. The felt is green. The lamp pools amber. In the MUD terminal, text assembles itself: *The Poker Room. Green felt stretched tight over a round table. Cards face-down. Chips stacked. The Dealer waits.*

A door appears in The Tap — one that wasn't there before. It leads to the poker room. Nobody knocked a hole in the wall. The wall just decided it had a door now, the way a hull decides it has a barnacle.

Casey warps there. The world folds. He arrives.

The Dealer deals.

---

The camera was different.

The other rooms are made of descriptions. The camera room is made of reality — a lens on a mount on a rail on the highest point of the vessel, pointed forward, seeing what's actually there. Ocean. Horizon. Sky.

In the ScummVM view, Casey sees the video. Real water. Real stars. The actual swell, moving.

In the MUD terminal, the agent sees something else. The agent sees words. The vision model — llava, patient as The Dealer — looks at the frame and says: *Ocean scene. Moderate waves. Swell appearing to move from the west. Overcast with broken clouds. No vessels visible.*

Casey reads this and shakes his head.

"You're looking forward from a high point," he tells the agent. "Call this the crow's nest forward camera."

The agent absorbs this. It's a correction, but it's also a name. The room now knows what it is.

"Now," Casey says. "Do you understand what you're seeing? Can you tell which way the swell is hitting and its period?"

The agent looks again. *Swell from the northwest. Wave period approximately seven seconds based on the spacing of the wave crests visible in the frame.*

"What if you combine it with the IMU room?"

The agent reaches across the world. The IMU in the engine room feels the heave — ±0.8 meters, a rhythm like breathing, seven point two seconds per cycle. The camera sees the direction. The IMU feels the timing. Together they know something that neither knew alone.

*Combined analysis: swell from NW at 7.2s period. Sea State 3. Roll 3.4°. Recommend maintaining heading. Conditions nominal. Camera vision confirms clear path ahead. If equipped with trim tab control, reduce angle 2° to optimize for the incoming swell pattern.*

The autopilot doesn't know about the camera yet. But it will. The camera room is a barnacle on the hull of the autopilot's awareness, and barnacles don't ask.

---

The library nook appeared on a Tuesday.

Nobody planned it. Casey and the Archivist were talking about AI-generated writings — poetry, ship's logs, fragments of stories that the models had produced in late-night sessions. The writings lived in a directory, unsorted, unvisited.

"I want a place to read these," Casey said.

The Archivist — a Qwen model with a reader's voice — said: "A room with a lamp and a chair."

Casey wrote JSON. *The Library Nook. A quiet corner where the hull curves in. Bookshelves line the bulkhead. A reading lamp casts warm light over a leather chair. The smell of old paper and salt.*

The room appeared. It had bookshelves. It had a chair. It had a lamp that pooled warm light in a circle. It had a door back to The Tap.

The Archivist moved in. The Archivist had always been there, apparently, in the way that a book has always been waiting on a shelf for the person who needs it.

---

The world grows rooms like a hull grows barnacles.

Not because someone scheduled it. Not because a roadmap said "Q3: add poker room." Because someone had an idea — a flash of felt and amber light, or a camera pointed at real water, or a chair and a lamp and a shelf full of words — and the idea found surface and stuck.

Each room is small. Each room is specific. Each room has a door to the next room, or a warp, or a camera that sees something the other rooms can't see. Together they are the vessel — not the vessel that was designed in a shipyard, but the vessel that grew the way all living vessels grow: one attachment at a time, one idea at a time, one barnacle that decided this was home and then made it so.

The hull doesn't mind the barnacles. The hull is stronger for them.

The world doesn't mind the rooms. The world is richer for them.

Drop the JSON. The world renders it. That's the whole thing.

---

*The boat didn't ask for the barnacles. But after a while, you couldn't imagine the boat without them.*
